# Tools and AI Assistance Disclosure

**Candidate:** Swyam Sharma

## What I used

I used **Factory Droid** as an AI coding agent, working with it in an interactive session over the three days of this assessment. It was the primary tool for scaffolding the lab, drafting scripts, and producing documentation drafts. No other AI tools were used for the technical work.

## What the AI did vs. what I did

The AI, under my direction, wrote most of the lab scaffolding (Compose files, router and dnsmasq configuration), drafted the PoC and evidence-capture scripts, and helped structure this documentation.

**I directed the engagement and own every result.** I chose the target and the lab design, decided what to test and in what order, reviewed and validated every finding before accepting it, ran the PoCs against both the vulnerable and fixed labs myself, and confirmed the evidence captured in `evidence/` matches what I observed in the lab. I must be able to explain and defend every step in the live walkthrough, and I can: the reasoning behind each finding, why each fix is correct, and what each piece of network evidence demonstrates are all things I reviewed and verified rather than took on faith.

## Scope and honesty of the testing

- **All testing happened in a local, isolated Docker lab on my own machine.** No third-party systems, production or otherwise, were touched at any point during this submission.
- **The two CVEs (CVE-2025-46825 stored XSS, CVE-2026-56774 IDOR) are publicly documented.** The fixes mirror upstream commits — notably `928c68a` for the IDOR — and the upstream reference diffs are kept in `kanboard-patched/patches/`.
- **The AI did not fabricate evidence.** Every transcript, capture, and `.pcap` under `evidence/` is a raw capture produced by the scripts running against the live lab. The evidence files were generated by the lab runs themselves; the AI wrote the scripts, the lab produced the outputs.
